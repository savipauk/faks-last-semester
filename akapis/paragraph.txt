The University of Zagreb is the oldest and largest university in Croatia, and one of the oldest in Southeast Europe. Its origins go back to 23 September 1669, when Emperor Leopold I granted the Jesuit academy in Zagreb university status. The institution was reorganized as a modern university in 1874 and has grown steadily since. Today it has around 70,000 students across more than thirty faculties, art academies and university departments. Its programs cover the natural sciences, engineering, medicine, the humanities, the social sciences and the arts. As Croatia's leading research center, the university produces a large share of the country's scientific output and works with partner institutions throughout Europe. It is also part of European exchange programs such as Erasmus+, which bring international students and researchers to Zagreb. With a history of more than 350 years and a strong place in Croatian public life, the University of Zagreb remains central to the nation's education, culture and science.
